What you are looking at

A replay of nine recent nights (7 foggy, 2 dry) showing how wet streets may have been along a few skate routes. A proof of concept on a 20 m (66 ft) grid, not a forecast.

How it works

  1. Terrain and buildings. USGS elevation plus 177,000 building footprints raised to their 2023 lidar roof height. Simple boxes, not a lidar scan.
  2. Wind. One steady west wind over the whole city on 20 m cells, scaled to each night's wind speed. About 98% of buildings lie inside 297 finer 5 m patches (2.5 m in Potrero) that correct street-level wind.
  3. Fog. A fog tracer drifts through that wind in four height layers and settles on the ground. Each night's fog top is estimated, so hills can rise above shallow fog.
  4. Sun and shade. Each street point gets a skyline in 72 directions, so the real sun position says sunlit or shaded. Open sky drives night cooling.
  5. Weather. Hourly Open-Meteo history for one SF point: temperature, dew point, wind, low cloud, rain, sunshine.

How the percentage is computed

Each street side starts at 0 at 6 PM. Every 10 minutes:

wetness += dew + fog + rain − evaporation − sun (kept between 0 and 100)

TermDriven byMain limit
+ DewRoad cooled below dew point (open sky, clear, calm)One weather point
+ FogFog at that street's height and distance inland, wind, porosityWest wind only
+ RainRain rate, less on porous pavingNot measured
− EvaporationDry air and windSimple rule
− SunMostly when the street is sunlitNo trees or cars

0 dry, 20 damp, 50 slick, 80 wet, 100 icy-fog. Rates are hand-set, not calibrated: it is a relative index, not a probability or a measured water film.

Limits

  • West wind only; offshore winds bring no fog.
  • Street-level wind correction exists only inside the patches, over-damped and uncalibrated.
  • One weather point for the whole city.
  • Never compared with measured wet roads.
  • No trees, parked cars or pavement heat storage.
  • Not a forecast or safety advice: judge the road yourself.
